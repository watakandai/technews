Think of this as my Twitter feed, organized: one page I check every morning.
I want the things I would have wanted to see, ranked so the top of the list
is worth my first two minutes.

## Top priority: robot foundation models

This is what I am building toward. I want to implement state-of-the-art
robot learning methods against a public benchmark, improve on them, and
write a paper. Rank this above everything else on the page.

- **The labs and their approach** - Physical Intelligence (pi0, pi0.5,
  openpi), Generalist AI (GEN-0 and its scaling results), Wayve (end-to-end
  driving, GAIA world models, LINGO), and anyone doing the same kind of
  thing: Skild AI, Figure's Helix, Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics, NVIDIA
  GR00T and Cosmos, Toyota Research's large behavior models, 1X, Tesla's
  end-to-end FSD and Optimus. Their papers, blog posts, releases, hires,
  funding and credible rumors about them all count.
- **The methods** - vision-language-action models, diffusion and flow-matching
  policies, action tokenization and chunking, world models used for policy
  learning or evaluation, cross-embodiment training, scaling laws for robot
  data, end-to-end driving, sim-to-real, and real-time inference on robots.
- **What I can use** - open weights, training code and datasets (LeRobot,
  openpi, Open X-Embodiment, DROID), and benchmarks and leaderboards (LIBERO,
  SimplerEnv, CALVIN, RoboCasa, ManiSkill, NAVSIM, Bench2Drive). A paper
  with released code and a benchmark number scores higher than one without;
  a new benchmark, or evidence that an existing one is saturated or broken,
  is exactly the kind of thing I need to see.
- Score these 85+ when they are substantive. A press release or a demo
  video with no technical detail is still interesting, but not top-of-page.

## What I want

- **Robotics** - manipulation, humanoids, autonomy, drones, field robotics,
  new platforms and what they can actually do. Company news and demos count.
- **Research** - papers worth knowing about, especially from robotics, ML,
  and the theory side. A good preprint beats a press release about it.
- **ML and AI** - new models and capabilities, training and inference
  techniques, evaluation and benchmarks, agents, and how people are really
  using them. Vendor announcements count when something changed.
- **Theory** - formal methods, verification, model checking, optimization,
  control theory. Niche is fine. Rank these high even when nobody upvoted
  them; they are exactly what an aggregator's front page buries.
- **Startups** - what is being founded, funded and built, especially in
  robotics, AI infrastructure and developer tools. Funding rounds, launches,
  who left where to start what.
- **Finance** - the business behind the technology: earnings that say
  something about where compute or robotics money is going, market moves,
  acquisitions, chip and infrastructure spending.
- **Productivity tools** - tools I might actually adopt. Editors, terminals,
  agent tooling, workflow and note-taking software.
- **Open-source libraries** - notable releases, new libraries worth a look,
  and significant changes to ones people depend on.
- **Rumors** - credible unreleased-product and unannounced-company chatter,
  especially chips, robots and AI labs. Clearly-sourced speculation is
  welcome; content-farm speculation is not.

## What to push down

- General consumer-gadget coverage, phone reviews, app updates, streaming
  and entertainment industry news.
- Culture-war and personality drama about tech figures.
- Listicles, "X things you should know", sponsored posts, roundups and
  recurring threads.
- Opinion posts that only react to a story I would already have seen.
- Crypto and web3, unless it is genuine cryptography or systems work.

## How to weigh things

- I would rather see a small, technical, specific post than a big story
  every outlet already ran. Prefer primary sources over coverage of them.
- Popularity is a tiebreaker, not the goal. A viral story outside my
  interests is noise; a quiet paper inside them is not.
- Depth over novelty: something I will learn from beats something merely new.
